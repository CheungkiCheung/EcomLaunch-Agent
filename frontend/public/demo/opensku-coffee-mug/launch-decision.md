# Sample Launch Decision — Compact Travel Coffee Mug

> Recorded OpenSKU demo fixture. This file demonstrates the delivery format only. It is not current market research, does not contain live marketplace data, and must not be used as a sourcing or inventory decision without fresh validation.

## Recommendation

**VALIDATE BEFORE COMMITTING**

The sample brief describes a plausible commuter product, but the most important purchase claims—leak resistance, one-hand operation, cleaning effort, cup-holder fit, and weight—depend on physical performance. The next step is a low-cost validation sprint, not an inventory order.

## Decision summary

| Question                              | Sample conclusion                                                               | Confidence                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Is the audience understandable?       | Yes: commuters and frequent travelers have a clear use occasion.                | Medium, based on the brief only |
| Is the position differentiated?       | Not yet: “compact” and “leak-resistant” are category claims until demonstrated. | High                            |
| Is the proposed $24–$34 range viable? | Unknown: price acceptance must be tested against an actual design and offer.    | Low                             |
| What is the largest risk?             | Product performance failing the promise under real commuting conditions.        | High                            |

## Required gates

Proceed to a small pilot only if all four gates pass:

1. **Leak test:** zero visible leakage after the defined bag-motion and inversion protocol.
2. **Usability test:** at least 8 of 10 target users can open, drink, close, and clean it without guidance.
3. **Message test:** the winning landing-page concept reaches the pre-agreed signup threshold without unsupported claims.
4. **Economics check:** landed cost, fulfilment, returns allowance, and acquisition assumptions leave the required contribution margin.

## Stop conditions

- Stop if the physical sample cannot pass the leak protocol after one design revision.
- Stop if users consistently describe cleaning or lid assembly as difficult.
- Reposition instead of scaling if interest depends on a claim the sample cannot reliably prove.
- Do not use scarcity, review counts, certifications, or performance statistics that have not been observed and documented.

## What would change this decision

Fresh competitor evidence, an engineering test report, prototype user sessions, a message-test result, and a complete unit-economics sheet would materially increase confidence.
